Code and pretrained models

Introduction

Accurate state estimation is at the heart of agile drone flight. While cameras are commonly used for localization, they fail in low light, high-speed motion, or textureless environments. Inertial odometry — using only IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit) data — offers a lightweight and robust alternative, but it often suffers from drift over time due to sensor noise and bias.

In this project, I explored how far we can push IMU-only odometry by extending a recent learning-based inertial odometry (IMO) framework for autonomous drone racing. The core idea was to make the learned model “aware” of the drone’s underlying physics — not just what it feels through accelerations and rotations, but also the forces and torques acting on its body.

Method

IMO feeds a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) with IMU and thrust data; the network predicts the drone's displacement over the next 0.5 s, and an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) fuses these predictions with the IMU for continuous pose estimation. EX-IMO changes three things:

  • Body frame. The network predicts the displacement in the drone's own frame instead of the world frame, so it cannot learn the absolute headings of the training tracks.
  • Torque input. The body-frame torques from the full quadrotor dynamics are added as an input, so the network sees how the vehicle is being driven, not only how it moves.
  • Thrust from the rotors. The collective thrust comes from a per-platform fit of the rotor signals, which is available in flight.

Results

In the paper, we validated the approach on the Blackbird and DIDO drone flight datasets and deployed it on a real racing quadrotor. The results show up to 61% improvement in relative error compared to the original IMO on unseen trajectories — demonstrating that embedding physical insight into learning models enhances robustness and transferability.

Open reproduction on four public UAV datasets

With the code release, we re-ran the comparison from scratch on four more public datasets (AI-IO, NanoBench, TII-RATM and NeuroBEM): 3 seeds per model, the filter tuned on validation data only, and whole groups of trajectory shapes held out of training. On trajectories never seen in training, EX-IMO lowers velocity error in all 8 held-out sets, by up to 72%, and position error in 5 of 8, by up to 80%.

EX-IMO vs base IMO on held-out trajectories, mean of 3 seeds. Faded bars are not significant (paired t-test p ≥ 0.05). Full tables in the repository.

What the reproduction shows:

  • Torque from the rotors carries velocity information. On its own it explains up to 94% of the variance of the body-frame displacement, likely because the rotors trim against aerodynamic moments that grow with airspeed. Torque computed from the gyro adds nothing the network does not already see.
  • The world-frame baseline memorises track headings. Rotating the TII-RATM flights by 90° in yaw raises base IMO's validation error from 0.34 to 5.8 m/s; the body-frame models do not change.
  • Position error is mixed on small datasets. On TII-RATM, with only 6 training flights per fold, neither model generalises well across tracks, and EX-IMO's position error is higher on the ellipse.

These numbers come from cleaned copies of the datasets that the repository builds, so they are not directly comparable with numbers published on the original files.

This work contributes to the ongoing effort to make autonomous drones faster, smaller, and more resilient — capable of navigating even when vision fails.
